## Applied editorial decisions

1. Remove unmeasured “hours saved,” “reduced support requests,” “prevented recurrence” and universal AI-citation claims.
2. Put current KLA work before the older roles. Do not turn current development into released or hardware-proven work.
3. Use the plain title “Software Engineer.” Explain semiconductor equipment, robotic vision and V2X in context.
4. Prefer the port-conflict diagnosis to abstract claims about debugging skill.
5. Describe Bosch work as a demonstrator/prototype. Safety-related subject matter does not establish safety certification expertise.
6. Keep project technology and user purpose; drop architecture catalogues and benchmark numbers without reproducible context.
7. Keep training separate from degrees. Confirm what was awarded before final PDF export.
8. Separate editorial questions from public-facing copy. A design example can be marked as a draft; the production interface must not display internal review instructions.

## Suggestions not adopted

The suggested headline “I build software that connects applications, devices and physical systems” adds abstract nouns without making the introduction more personal. Prefer a direct name, role and current-work sentence.

One proposed Pickit rewrite grouped three separate tools into a single long bullet. Keep the concrete diagnosis, client and log workflow individually scannable.

No claim that columns categorically fail ATS, that particular font formats always fail parsers, or that keyword repetition receives a known AI scoring weight is accepted. Check actual extraction from the generated PDF and the receiving application's fields.

## Outstanding questions

The eight user questions in the conversation remain authoritative. The reviews do not resolve personal facts. Publication readiness is blocked by unknown KLA header details, contribution boundaries, degree status and award scope; layout work can proceed using a clearly marked example.
