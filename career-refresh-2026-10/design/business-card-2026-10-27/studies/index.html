Five paper introductions.

One real identity, five distinct ways to put it on paper. Landscape business cards, with the contact information doing the work—not a monogram or a slogan.

These are screen studies, not photographs of printed stock. Grain, impression, edges and translucency are digital cues; every stock and finishing specification below is a proposal.

01

Cotton impression

A warm, typographic introduction. The name carries the weight.

85 × 55 mm · single face

Let the margin feel generous, and the introduction feel human.

Format
85 × 55 mm
Stock
Warm cotton, 600 gsm proposed
Ink
Soft charcoal; restrained impression
Type
Georgia / Figtree

Why it works

A serif name and quiet sans-serif details establish a clear, personal hierarchy. The two bottom anchors give the blank space purpose.

Watch

Exaggerated emboss shadows would make it look artificial. At small sizes, keep the contacts dark; do not turn them into pale “fine print.”

Strongest base for the website introduction: calm, legible, recognisably a business card.

90 × 50 mm
Stock
Forest / natural duplex proposed
Ink
Warm opaque light ink; proof first
Type
Figtree, medium / regular

Why it works

The stacked name and offset role are a different composition, not a colour swap. The horizontal contact rail makes the wider format feel deliberate.

Watch

Light ink on dark stock needs a physical proof. Heavy green coverage also feels more corporate; an all-dark web section would overstate it.

Best source of colour and edge detail. Borrow the restraint, not necessarily the dark face.

Photographic margin

A small portrait window; a full-width contact line.

Format
85 × 55 mm
Stock
Smooth natural white proposed

Supplied professional portrait
Type
Figtree, regular / medium

Why it works

The portrait has a real margin and a controlled crop. Contacts sit below both columns, so the email is not squeezed into a narrow picture caption.

Watch

The small photograph loses expression on a phone, and makes this closer to an editorial calling card. Never reduce type to preserve a larger image.

Useful for a portrait-led variant. In the final introduction, a separate companion print gives the face more room.

04

Swiss register

An explicit grid, a strong name, and one meaningful accent.

90 × 50 mm · single face

Page actions · off the paperContact ↗Website ↗

Make precision visible through alignment, not technical decoration.

Format
90 × 50 mm
Stock
Smooth uncoated white proposed
Ink
Charcoal and vermilion
Type
Figtree, semibold / regular

Why it works

The oversized name stays on one line at normal mobile sizes. C++ is the accent because it is actual information. The lower zones have different, useful jobs.

Watch

The compact 90 × 50 format is less forgiving of enlarged type. Let the lower columns reflow instead of forcing a rigid printed grid onto every reader.

Best information discipline. Excellent fallback behaviour, but cooler and less personal than cotton.

05

Vellum & portrait

A translucent contact face with a separate, purposeful photograph.

85 × 55 mm · card + print

Keep the introduction on one piece, the atmosphere on another.

Format
85 × 55 mm face; separate photo
Stock
Vellum / warm backing proposed
Image
Supplied window portrait
Type
Figtree, spaced capitals

Why it works

The centered contact face has a different rhythm. The photograph is an actual companion print, not an invented back or an excuse to hide essential information.

Watch

Translucency is the least reliable screen-to-print effect here. A light backing is essential. On mobile, a separate portrait below the card is better than a decorative overlap.

Borrow the two-piece idea, not the optical effect. The quiet card should also work without a photograph.

Pocket proof / no miniature type

Compare the small faces.

These are the same live cards, in 390 or 320 CSS-pixel frames with 16-pixel gutters. No image scaling, no alternate mobile identity.

On a screen narrower than the selected frame, the frame uses the available width. At enlarged text sizes the paper may become taller: readable contact information takes precedence over a fixed rectangle.

Recommendation from the original studies

Cotton, with a forest edge.

Use 01’s name-led hierarchy and 85 × 55 proportions. Take only the deep green ink and restrained edge from 02, and the alignment discipline of 04. Keep the paper warm and the contacts fully readable.

Put the professional portrait beside the card as a separate print, borrowing 05’s two-piece logic rather than its translucency. At mobile widths it belongs below the card, not underneath the email.

Keep CV, contact and vCard actions in the page interface. This study links only the supplied email and website; no unverified CV path or download has been invented.

This recommendation is based on the five original designs only. The separate five-reference review should validate or revise it before application integration. There is no sixth “final” card or reference claim hidden in this board.

Local design evidence only. All five faces use the same real identity and contact data. No monogram, slogan, flipping interaction, tracking or remote dependency. Figtree is supplied under the SIL Open Font License; serif type is the local Georgia system font.
