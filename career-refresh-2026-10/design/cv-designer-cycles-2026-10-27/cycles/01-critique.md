# Cycle 1 — identity and header composition

Before: `01-before-full.png`, `01-before-top.png`. These were freshly captured from the current HTML and viewed at full-page and top-detail scale.

## Designer critique

- **Identity/restraint:** the 34pt bold name and pale bordered rectangle read like an application notification. The 2mm inset is too tight for that visual weight.
- **Hierarchy:** contact information is a detached right block; the frame is more prominent than the professional role.
- **Rhythm:** the name/header consumes attention without creating a good transition into the first employer.
- **Composition:** the boxed shape adds a second page edge. The green left rule suggests UI status, not stationery.
- **Print/data:** one-page fit and valid links do not rescue those visual choices. Facts and print behavior are intact and must remain so.

## Revision decision

Remove the banner shape. Use a lighter, smaller editorial name treatment, an unboxed role and one aligned contact line. Keep the forest colour as a restrained cue. Leave the employment layout unchanged in this pass so the header change can be judged directly.

## After viewing the new captures

- The banner/notification reading is gone. The serif name has a quieter, more personal character and the contact line now belongs to the same composition.
- The page still has an obvious identity at actual scale; the name no longer needs a filled frame to stand out.
- The employment hierarchy is now the weakest part: company and role still run together, and uppercase green section labels compete with employer headings.
- Retain the unboxed identity. In cycle 2, separate the employer/date line from the role/context line.
- Print remains one A4 page with six links. That is a constraint check, not the reason this header is preferred.
